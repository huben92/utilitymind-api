# Wheel manufacturing model and synthetic history audit

This is an **illustrative aluminum alloy wheel factory**, not a validated model of a particular Wheelworks plant. All generated measurements are synthetic. Rates below are scenario inputs, not manufacturer specifications or measured site loads.

## Process audit

The original production flow covers raw material handling, charging, melting, rotary degassing, casting, T6 heat treatment, machining, and painting. That sequence is a reasonable *simplified* cast-wheel flow: [Dicastal North America](https://www.dicastalna.com/capabilities/) describes melting, casting, heat treatment, machining, and painting in its wheel process; [ALCAR](https://www.alcar-wheels.com/en/alcar-group/news/alcar-news/2024-dezent-production/) also describes casting, mechanical processing, pretreatment, and coating. The map omits inspection/testing and some auxiliary operations; Dicastal lists cooling, X-ray inspection, washing, balancing, and leak testing as distinct activities. These have **not** been invented as extra Wheelworks production machines without plant confirmation.

Before this update, 10 pilot meters were concentrated on the warehouse, charging area, and one melting furnace. Most mapped machines had no meter, so machine-level comparisons were not representative. Existing meter IDs, assignments, enabled states, configured baselines, maximums, and earlier telemetry were preserved to avoid rewriting their provenance. Their warehouse/charging utility assignments remain *pilot assumptions*, not verified installed meter locations. One user-added machine, `hello`, has an unknown process category and remains unmodeled; inventing its usage would make the history less credible.

## Demonstration catalog

The script adds stage-appropriate meters to the 23 seeded production machines and a separate **Utility Services** location with an air compressor, chiller, boiler, water pump, and backup generator. These systems are plausible support assets, not claims that the actual factory owns this exact equipment. Electricity and fuel inputs have `meter_role=consumption`; compressed air, chilled water, steam, clean water, and generator electricity outputs have `meter_role=supply`. Overview/category totals and reports count consumption meters only, avoiding addition of generation output to end-use consumption.

The modeled categories are electricity (`kW`), clean water/air/gas/chilled water (`m³/h`), diesel (`L/h`), and **steam** (`kg/h`). Steam and hot water are distinct media; the demonstration includes steam only. Casting cooling and painting pretreatment/steam use are explicit demonstration assumptions requiring confirmation from equipment drawings. The [U.S. Department of Energy's metalcasting assessment](https://www1.eere.energy.gov/manufacturing/resources/metalcasting/pdfs/implementation_final.pdf) supports compressed-air uses around dies and post-casting operations; it does not specify loads for this factory.

## History and baseline method

- The seed covers **2026-07-18 04:00 UTC through 2026-10-18 04:00 UTC**, exclusive, at five-minute intervals: **1,960,704 readings for 74 meters**. Every row has `simulated=true` and topic `synthetic-history/wheelworks-v1`. It ends more than an hour before generation time and never enters the MQTT ingest path, so the import itself cannot create live alerts.
- The generator models weekday operation 06:00–22:00, reduced Saturday operation 06:00–14:00, Sunday idle, painting 07:00–20:00, furnace standby, a weekly backup-generator test, equipment variation, and utility supply tracking of end-use demand. These are scenario assumptions, not actual schedules or production output.
- For every meter and local weekday/15-minute slot, the app calculates a median expected rate and a conservative band from the synthetic readings. Every profile slot has 13–14 distinct historical days and around 39–42 five-minute samples. The band is the lower/upper empirical quantile with a small margin. This is a **time-matched simulated baseline**, not a production-adjusted engineering target or a safe operating limit.
- Charts show the historical expected trend as a dashed line. Configured master-data baselines stay separate and unchanged; operational maximums stay separate. A fresh MQTT reading above or below the historical band opens a distinct review notification, which closes after the reading returns inside the band. The notice is an advisory deviation, not proof of leakage, shortage, or a fault.
- Historical imports are resumable after a complete telemetry load and idempotent once the run is recorded. Daily/monthly reporting treats five-minute synthetic samples as five-minute coverage rather than using the live MQTT interval.

Before use with a real plant, confirm machine inventory, meter locations and direction, rated capacities, utility boundaries, actual operating schedules and shifts, wheel output, and the quality of physical readings. Rebuild the profiles from verified measurements conditioned on production activity. The [DOE metalcasting energy study](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2013/11/f4/doebestpractice_052804.pdf) explains why furnace and process loads vary by equipment and operation; its values must not be copied as this site's baseline.
